# Irreducibility after reduction of an admissible sl2 lattice

## Original exercise

James E. Humphreys, *Introduction to Lie Algebras and Representation Theory*,
Graduate Texts in Mathematics 9, Springer, section 27, exercise 7,
printed page 164. The exercise-list convention on page 163 sets
\(L=\mathfrak{sl}_2\) and identifies weights with integers.
Book DOI: [10.1007/978-1-4612-6398-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-6398-2).

> 7. If \(0\leq\lambda<\operatorname{char}K\), \(V=V(\lambda)\),
> prove that \(V(K)\) is irreducible as \(L(K)\)-module.

## Definitions and complete target

Let \(p\) be any prime, \(K\) any field of characteristic \(p\), and
\(\lambda\) an integer with \(0\leq\lambda<p\).
Let \(V(\lambda)\) be the complex irreducible highest-weight
\(\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb C)\)-module of highest weight \(\lambda\),
of dimension \(\lambda+1\). Its Chevalley generators satisfy

\[
[h,x]=2x,\qquad[h,y]=-2y,\qquad[x,y]=h.
\]

Choose a nonzero highest-weight vector \(v\) with \(xv=0\) and
\(hv=\lambda v\). Let \(U_{\mathbb Z}\) be the integer subalgebra of
the universal enveloping algebra generated by all divided powers
\(X_r=x^r/r!\), \(Y_r=y^r/r!\), for integers \(r\geq0\).
An **admissible lattice** \(M\subset V(\lambda)\) is a free abelian
subgroup with an integer basis that is also a complex basis, stable under
\(U_{\mathbb Z}\). It is arbitrary, not necessarily the minimal lattice.

Put \(V(K)=M\otimes_{\mathbb Z}K\). The Lie algebra
\(L(K)=(\mathbb Zx+\mathbb Zy+\mathbb Zh)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}K\)
acts by the induced lattice operators. This is the Chevalley
\(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) construction also in characteristic 2.
Prove that \(V(K)\) is nonzero and every \(K\)-linear subspace
invariant under \(x,y,h\) is zero or all of \(V(K)\).
Both \(\lambda=0\) and \(p=2\) are included; \(K\) need not be
algebraically closed.

## Fixed natural-language proof

### 1. Divided-power coordinates in characteristic zero

Put \(n=\lambda+1\) and \(e_i=y^iv/i!\) for \(0\leq i\leq\lambda\).
The commutator identities give, by induction,

\[
hy^iv=(\lambda-2i)y^iv,\qquad
xy^iv=i(\lambda-i+1)y^{i-1}v\quad(i\geq1).
\]

For \(i\leq\lambda\), applying \(x^i\) to \(y^iv\) gives
\(i!\lambda(\lambda-1)\cdots(\lambda-i+1)v\ne0\) over \(\mathbb C\).
At \(i=0\) the empty product is one. Thus every \(e_i\ne0\).
Their \(h\)-eigenvalues \(\lambda-2i\) are distinct over \(\mathbb C\),
so they are linearly independent: applying
\(\prod_{k\ne j}(h-(\lambda-2k)\mathrm{Id})\) to a relation isolates
its \(j\)-th coefficient with a nonzero multiplier.
There are \(n\) vectors, the dimension of \(V(\lambda)\), hence they
form a basis. Also \(y^{\lambda+1}v=0\), since otherwise it would be
an eigenvector of eigenvalue \(-\lambda-2\), absent from this basis.
All higher lowering powers vanish as well.

The operator matrices are therefore

\[
xe_0=0,\quad xe_i=(\lambda-i+1)e_{i-1}\ (i\geq1),\qquad
ye_i=(i+1)e_{i+1}\ (i<\lambda),\quad ye_\lambda=0,
\quad he_i=(\lambda-2i)e_i.
\]

Iteration gives the integral divided-power matrices

\[
X_re_i=\binom{\lambda-i+r}{r}e_{i-r}\quad(0\leq r\leq i),
\qquad
Y_re_i=\binom{i+r}{r}e_{i+r}\quad(i+r\leq\lambda).       \tag{1}
\]

The respective expressions are zero when \(r>i\) or
\(i+r>\lambda\), by termination of the strings just proved.

### 2. Matrix units preserve the localized arbitrary lattice

Let \(R=\mathbb Z_{(p)}=\{a/b\in\mathbb Q:p\nmid b\}\), and regard
\(M_R=R\otimes_{\mathbb Z}M\) as a submodule of \(V(\lambda)\).
The embedding is injective because an integer basis of \(M\) is
complex-linearly independent. The module \(M_R\) is free of rank \(n\)
over \(R\) and is preserved by all \(X_r,Y_r\), by admissibility.

Define \(E_{ij}\) to send \(e_j\) to \(e_i\) and all other basis
vectors to zero. Formula (1) gives

\[
P:=Y_\lambda X_\lambda=E_{\lambda\lambda},\qquad
X_{\lambda-i}P=E_{i\lambda},\qquad
P Y_{\lambda-j}=\binom\lambda j E_{\lambda j}.
\]

For the last identity, an input index below \(j\) is sent below
\(e_\lambda\) and killed by \(P\); an index above \(j\) is sent past
the end of the lowering string. On \(e_j\) the coefficient is precisely
\(\binom\lambda j\). Since \(\lambda<p\), none of
\(\lambda!\), \(j!\), \((\lambda-j)!\) is divisible by \(p\), so
\(\binom\lambda j\) is a unit in \(R\). Consequently

\[
E_{ij}=\left(\binom\lambda j\right)^{-1}
       X_{\lambda-i}P Y_{\lambda-j}.                 \tag{2}
\]

Every matrix unit preserves \(M_R\). No assertion about distinct
weights modulo \(p\) has been used.

### 3. Recover a standard basis for the arbitrary lattice

Define \(J_i=\{c\in\mathbb C:c e_i\in M_R\}\).
The coordinate projection \(E_{ii}\) maps \(M_R\) onto \(J_i e_i\).
Applying \(E_{ij}\) to \(c e_j\) shows \(J_j\subseteq J_i\);
reversing the indices gives equality. Let \(J\) be their common module.
It is finitely generated, being a projection of a finite free module,
and nonzero because \(M_R\) spans \(V(\lambda)\) over \(\mathbb C\).
The coordinate projections and independence of the \(e_i\) give

\[
M_R=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\lambda}J e_i.                  \tag{3}
\]

One must still prove that \(J\) has rank one; containment in
\(\mathbb C\) alone does not imply that. Let
\(r=\dim_{\mathbb Q}(\mathbb Q\otimes_R J)\), which is finite.
Tensoring (3) with the fraction field \(\mathbb Q\) and using the
rank \(n\) of \(M_R\) gives \(n=nr\), so \(r=1\), since \(n>0\).
The multiplication map \(\mathbb Q\otimes_R J\to\mathbb C\) is
injective: this tensor product is the localization of the torsion-free
module \(J\subset\mathbb C\), and a fraction mapping to zero has
zero numerator. Thus \(J\) spans a one-dimensional rational line.

Choose \(0\ne a\in J\) and write a finite set of nonzero generators
as \(q_1a,\ldots,q_ka\) with \(q_j\in\mathbb Q^\times\).
Each \(q_j=p^{\nu_j}u_j\) for \(\nu_j\in\mathbb Z\) and a unit
\(u_j\) of \(R\). Let \(\nu=\min_j\nu_j\). All generators belong
to \(p^\nu R a\), and a generator with minimal exponent generates
that whole module. Therefore \(J=Rb\) for a nonzero \(b\in J\).
By (3), \(b e_0,\ldots,b e_\lambda\) is an \(R\)-basis of \(M_R\).

### 4. Reduce and extend scalars

All denominators in \(R\) are invertible in \(K\), so the integer map
extends to \(R\to K\). The natural tensor maps give

\[
M\otimes_{\mathbb Z}K\cong M_R\otimes_R K
\cong(M_R/pM_R)\otimes_{\mathbb F_p}K.
\]

For example the first map sends \(m\otimes c\) to
\((1\otimes m)\otimes c\); its inverse sends
\((r\otimes m)\otimes c\) to \(m\otimes\bar r c\).
The reduced images \(b_0,\ldots,b_\lambda\) of \(b e_i\) form a
\(K\)-basis, with exactly the matrices of step 1 reduced into \(K\).
The matrix-unit identities (2) persist on this basis.

For \(0\leq r\leq\lambda<p\), \(r!\ne0\) in \(K\), and the induced
\(X_r,Y_r\) equal \((r!)^{-1}x^r,(r!)^{-1}y^r\). Hence any
\(K\)-subspace invariant under \(x,y\) is invariant under every matrix
unit in (2).

### 5. Conclude irreducibility

Let \(W\) be invariant. If it is nonzero, choose
\(0\ne w=\sum_i c_i b_i\in W\), and an index \(j\) with \(c_j\ne0\).
For every \(i\), \(E_{ij}w=c_jb_i\in W\). Division by \(c_j\)
gives \(b_i\in W\), so all basis vectors lie in \(W\) and
\(W=V(K)\). Since \(\dim_K V(K)=\lambda+1>0\), it is nonzero and
irreducible. If \(\lambda=0\), all relevant factorials and binomial
coefficients are one and the argument applies unchanged. For \(p=2\),
the allowed \(\lambda\) are zero and one; the proof still works
without requiring distinct reduced \(h\)-eigenvalues.
