Global route and notation. The full target and all original hypotheses H001–H005 are retained. H002, the unit length of q, is retained but is not needed for the energy argument. The approved interpretation of compact support is uniform compact support of u-q on each closed positive-time interval, never compact support of u. No derivative, measurability, or coherent selection of the pointwise normality scalar is assumed. There is no factor 1/2 in e or E.

Use X=R^n and Y=R^m with their Euclidean inner products, and Lebesgue measure on X. In Lean X and Y may be EuclideanSpace R (Fin n) and EuclideanSpace R (Fin m), with indices 0,…,n-1 in place of 1,…,n; these are just relabelings of the same standard orthonormal coordinates and volume. On Z=X×R let Ω={(x,t):t>0}. Extend u arbitrarily outside Ω, for example by q. This is only a total representation: Ω is open, and every positive-time point has a neighborhood contained in Ω, so all derivatives there depend on the original germ by B009. There is no smoothness assertion across t=0.

For a constant direction v in a real normed domain define D_v F(z)=fderiv R F z v. Put T=(0,1) and S_j=(b_j,0), with b_j the jth standard basis vector of X. Write V=D_T u=u_t, W_j=D_{S_j}u=∂_{x_j}u, A=D_T V=u_tt and B_j=D_{S_j}W_j=∂_{x_jx_j}u. To identify a direction derivative with the indicated ordinary partial derivative, apply the chain rule to h↦z+h v, whose derivative is v. At a positive-time point the curve stays in Ω for small h. Define e=|V|²+Σ_j|W_j|² and g_j=⟨V,W_j⟩.

R001: Fix x and t>0. The curve r↦u(x,r) is smooth near t, so B005 gives the derivative of its squared norm as 2⟨u,u_t⟩. Its squared norm equals the constant 1 near t by H003. Local equality to a constant gives zero derivative (B009, or its one-dimensional evaluation), and uniqueness of the derivative gives 2⟨u,u_t⟩=0. Divide by 2. At the given point eliminate H004's existential witness a. By real inner-product symmetry and scalar bilinearity,
⟨u_t,u_tt-Σ_j∂_{x_jx_j}u⟩=⟨u_t,a u⟩=a⟨u_t,u⟩=0.
This use is pointwise and introduces no scalar field with extra regularity.

R002: B008 on the open set Ω gives smoothness of the derivative field. Evaluation of a continuous linear map at a fixed vector preserves smoothness (it is a continuous linear operation); repeating gives smoothness of V,W_j,A,B_j and all higher constant-direction derivatives used here. Inner products and squared norms in finite-dimensional Euclidean coordinates are finite sums of products of coordinate functions, so e,g_j and their derivatives and the stated inner-product terms are smooth. Their fixed-time spatial sections are smooth by the affine inclusion x↦(x,t).

Fix arbitrary t0>0. Apply H005 with s=t0/2 and t=3t0/2 to obtain a compact C. Compact subsets of Euclidean space are closed. Consequently O=(X\C)×(t0/2,3t0/2) is open and lies in Ω, and u=q throughout O. At every z∈O, u agrees near z with the constant q. B009 and the derivative of a constant give Du(z)=0. This is true on the whole open O, so the same argument applied to the derivative field gives zero second derivatives, and repetition gives every positive-order derivative zero on O. Each term listed in N002 contains positive-order derivatives of u, so all vanish there. At fixed interior time r their nonzero sets are contained in C; since C is closed their topological supports are also contained in C and are compact. No endpoint-time derivative vanishing is inferred from this window.

R003: Smoothness and B008 ensure all functions differentiated below are differentiable on Ω. For the needed mixed-partial identification set L(z)=fderiv R u z. Differentiating the continuous bilinear evaluation L(z)w at constant w gives D_v(D_w u)(z)=(fderiv R L z v)w; the term involving a derivative of w is zero. B007 applied to u, which is C² at every z∈Ω, gives equality of this expression with (fderiv R L z w)v. Thus D_T W_j=D_{S_j}V. This step uses only smoothness near z, not outside Ω.
Apply B005 along time sections to V and each W_j, differentiate the finite sum, and substitute this mixed identity:
∂_t e=2⟨V,A⟩+2Σ_j⟨W_j,D_{S_j}V⟩.
Apply B006 to the spatial section of g_j, or equivalently in direction S_j in Z:
∂_{x_j}g_j=⟨V,B_j⟩+⟨D_{S_j}V,W_j⟩.
Symmetry of the real inner product and finite-sum algebra therefore give
∂_t e-2Σ_j∂_{x_j}g_j=2⟨V,A-Σ_j B_j⟩=0
by N001. This proves N003 with the exact stated factor 2.

R005: Fix t0>0. From N002 choose the common compact C and let U=(t0/2,3t0/2). The uncurried real functions F(r,x)=e(x,r) and D(r,x)=∂_t e(x,r) are jointly continuous on U×X. For every x and every r∈U, HasDerivAt (s↦F(s,x)) D(r,x) r holds by the definition of ∂_t e and smoothness. Their spatial sections are integrable by N004 and hence almost everywhere strongly measurable. They vanish outside C throughout U.

Choose R>0 large enough that C lies in the compact cube Q=[-R,R]^n; boundedness of compact subsets of finite-dimensional Euclidean space ensures this. The compact time interval L=[3t0/4,5t0/4] lies in U. The continuous |D| has a finite bound B≥0 on L×Q by compactness. Let b(x)=B 1_Q(x). The cube Q is closed, measurable and has finite Lebesgue measure (2R)^n, hence b is integrable. For every r in the open neighborhood s=(3t0/4,5t0/4) of t0 and every x∈Q the derivative bound |D(r,x)|≤b(x) holds by construction; for x∉Q it holds because x∉C and D(r,x)=0. Thus the bound holds everywhere and hence almost everywhere, uniformly in the same neighborhood s. The derivative hypothesis also holds everywhere on s. Section measurability holds eventually at t0 since U is a neighborhood of t0; F(t0,·) is integrable and D(t0,·) is almost everywhere strongly measurable by N004. These check every premise of B002 with spatial X, F,D,t0,s,b. Its conclusion gives
HasDerivAt (r↦∫_X e(x,r) dx) (∫_X ∂_t e(x,t0) dx) t0.
Any arbitrary extension of F,D to nonpositive times is immaterial, since all these assertions are local to U; the integral agrees with the actual energy function near t0. This proves N005 for the actual finite integral.

Adapter B004 (recursive class 3 derivation): If g is C¹ with compact support, g is continuous and differentiable everywhere. The C¹ characterization gives continuity of its Frechet derivative, and evaluation at fixed v is continuous. Off tsupport g, the function agrees locally with zero, so its derivative is zero. Hence tsupport(x↦D_v g(x))⊆tsupport g, also compact. B001 gives integrability of g and D_v g. In B003 use f=1 and the given g. The three required integrands are, respectively, zero, D_v g, and g, all integrable. A constant is differentiable at every point of tsupport g, and C¹ g is differentiable at every point of tsupport 1. The conclusion is ∫D_v g=-∫0=0. The prepared Lean proof may use the exact native lemma HasCompactSupport.fderiv_apply for the support inclusion; it proves this same neighborhood argument. No integrability of the constant 1 is needed.

R006: Fix t>0 and j. By N002 the spatial section x↦g_j(x,t) is smooth, hence C¹, with compact topological support. Apply B004 with v=b_j. The derivative of this spatial section evaluated at b_j is ∂_{x_j}g_j(x,t) by the directional chain rule for the affine inclusion. Thus ∫_X ∂_{x_j}g_j(x,t) dx=0. ∫_X ∂_t e(x,t) dx=2Σ_j∫_X ∂_{x_j}g_j(x,t) dx=0.
