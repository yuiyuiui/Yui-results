B101. Derive the characteristic-zero strings and all required coordinates.
Write t_r=y^r v. From hy=yh−2y and xy=yx+h, induction gives for every natural r
  h t_r=(λ−2r)t_r,
  x t_0=0,
  x t_r=r(λ−r+1)t_{r−1} for r≥1.
Indeed the h induction subtracts 2 from the weight each time. If x t_r=r(λ−r+1)t_{r−1}, then
  x t_{r+1}=y x t_r+h t_r
            =[r(λ−r+1)+λ−2r]t_r
            =(r+1)(λ−r)t_r.
The same computation starts at r=0 using xv=0, hv=λv. Scalar coefficients here are in ℂ, with integer arithmetic.

For 0≤i≤λ, iteration of this formula gives
  x^i t_i = [∏_{s=1}^i s(λ−s+1)]v
          = i!·[λ(λ−1)...(λ−i+1)]v.
Each factor is a positive integer, hence a nonzero complex number, and v≠0. For i=0 the product is 1. Thus t_i≠0. Put e_i=t_i/i!; these are nonzero eigenvectors of h with weights λ−2i. These weights are injectively indexed over ℂ, since equality gives 2(i−j)=0 and characteristic zero gives i=j. B001 proves their linear independence; B002 and dim V=n show they span. The given finite dimension supplies the finite-dimensionality required by B002.

It remains to justify termination rather than assume it. The vector t_{λ+1} has weight −λ−2 by the same h induction. Write it uniquely in the spanning independent family e_i. Its eigenvector equation gives that its i-th coefficient times
  (λ−2i)−(−λ−2)=2(λ+1−i)
is zero. This factor is a nonzero complex number for every i≤λ. Every coefficient is zero, so t_{λ+1}=0. All further t_r vanish by another application of y.

Dividing the earlier formulas by i! now gives
  x e_0=0; x e_i=(λ−i+1)e_{i−1} (1≤i≤λ);
  y e_i=(i+1)e_{i+1} (i<λ); y e_λ=0;
  h e_i=(λ−2i)e_i.
Iteration gives, for all natural r,
  X_r e_i=binom(λ−i+r,r)e_{i−r} if r≤i, and 0 if r>i;
  Y_r e_i=binom(i+r,r)e_{i+r} if i+r≤λ, and 0 otherwise.
For the first nonzero case the numerator accumulated by x^r is
(λ−i+1)...(λ−i+r)=(λ−i+r)!/(λ−i)!; division by r! gives the displayed binomial by B005. For the second it is (i+1)...(i+r)=(i+r)!/i!, again giving the binomial by B005. All divisions in this paragraph occur only in ℂ, where the factorials are nonzero. In the remaining cases the iterate reaches x e_0=0 or y e_λ=0 before the required number of steps. This proves every clause of B101, including r=0.

B102. Construct integer endomorphisms with cleared matrix-unit identities.
Let E_ij be the complex-linear map e_j↦e_i, e_k↦0 for k≠j, and let c_j=binom(λ,j), a natural integer. Formula B005 shows c_j j!(λ−j)!=λ!, so c_j>0; it also gives binom(λ,λ−j)=c_j by swapping the factorial factors and cancelling their positive product.

Set P=Y_λ X_λ. If k<λ, X_λ e_k=0. On e_λ, X_λ e_λ=e_0 and Y_λ e_0=e_λ. Thus P=E_λλ. The B101 formulas also give X_{λ−i}e_λ=e_i with coefficient 1. To compute P Y_{λ−j}, start with e_k. If k<j, Y_{λ−j}e_k is a multiple of e_{k+λ−j}, whose index is below λ, and P kills it. If k>j, that Y operator is zero by termination. If k=j, the value before P is binom(λ,λ−j)e_λ=c_j e_λ. Consequently
  A_ij := X_{λ−i} P Y_{λ−j}
         = X_{λ−i} Y_λ X_λ Y_{λ−j}
         = c_j E_ij                                                   (a)
as complex-linear endomorphisms.

Admissibility ensures every factor of A_ij preserves M, so A_ij restricts to a ℤ-linear endomorphism of M. The E_ij themselves are NOT asserted to preserve M. In End_ℂ(V), evaluation on e_t gives
  E_ij E_kl=E_il if j=k, and 0 if j≠k;  ∑_i E_ii=Id.
Using (a), it follows that
  A_ij A_kl=c_j A_il if j=k, and 0 if j≠k.              (b)
Define the natural integers
  C=∏_{t∈I} c_t,   d_i=∏_{t∈I,t≠i} c_t.
The product with no factors is 1. Finite product decomposition gives C=c_i d_i for each i, and hence
  ∑_{i∈I} d_i A_ii = C Id.                            (c)
Equations (b) and (c) use only integer scalars and endomorphisms preserving M. For any m∈M their two sides have the same image in V by the complex identities. The injectivity of ι then proves the equalities ON M. This equality argument uses no tensor-injectivity claim and no rational scalar action on M.

Likewise r! X_r=x^r and r! Y_r=y^r, initially equalities in End_ℂ(V), restrict to equalities of ℤ-endomorphisms of M for every r. Since X_1=x, Y_1=y preserve M, the commutator xy−yx also preserves M, and its restriction is the h action. These statements prove B102.

B103. Extend the integer identities to the original reduced module.

T is nonzero: for example β_0≠0 by independence of a basis over the nontrivial field K. This proof does not use a reduction of the complex coordinate basis e_i.

For every ℤ-endomorphism f of M, use B007 for its extension f̂. The basis elements 1⊗b_i span T, and indeed all pure tensors c⊗m span: write m=∑_i z_i b_i with z_i∈ℤ and expand by the balanced tensor relations. The formula f̂(c⊗m)=c⊗f(m) proves that scalar extension preserves addition, integer scalar multiplication (with the integer cast into K), composition and the identity. For composition, f̂ ĝ(c⊗m)=c⊗f(g(m)); for sums it is c⊗(f(m)+g(m)); for an integer z, c⊗(z f(m))=(zc)⊗f(m), the balanced relation. Equality on the displayed spanning tensors proves equality of the K-linear maps. Finite sums and powers follow by induction. Thus no assumed faithful scalar extension, flatness, or unspecified ring map on endomorphisms is needed.

For r≤λ<p, B004 implies the cast of r! into K is nonzero. In particular the cast of λ! is nonzero. For j≤λ, cast B005 to K:

If c̄_j were zero, the left side would be zero, a contradiction. Hence c̄_j≠0 for every j, and C̄=∏ c̄_j≠0, since a field has no zero divisors.

B104. Prove the matrix-unit simplicity lemma.
Let F be any field, n>0, dim_F U=n, and F_ij (i,j∈Fin(n)) satisfy (d) and the multiplication relations in the B104 statement. Let W be a subspace invariant under them. If W=0 the desired alternative holds. Otherwise choose w∈W with w≠0. Equation (d), evaluated at w, gives ∑_j F_jj w=w. Hence at least one j has u=F_jj w≠0; if all terms were zero their sum would be zero. Invariance gives u∈W, and F_jj u=u by idempotence. Put u_i=F_ij u for all i∈Fin(n). Each u_i∈W.

For a relation ∑_i a_i u_i=0 and fixed k∈Fin(n), apply F_jk. The product relation says
  F_jk u_i=F_jk F_ij u=0 if k≠i,
  F_jk u_k=F_jj u=u.
Thus a_k u=0. As u≠0 and F is a field, a_k=0 (otherwise multiply by its inverse). All coefficients vanish, proving linear independence of the finite family u_i. Its cardinality is n=dim_F U, so B002 makes its span all of U. Since W contains every u_i it contains their span, and W=U. This proves the universal dichotomy in B104 without a hypothesis of algebraic closure.
