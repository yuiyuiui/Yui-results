We prove the exact full_target with its original assumptions: n is any natural number (zero is allowed), E⊆ℝ^n is nonempty and compact, and I=[0,1]. We model Euclidean ℝ^n by Ambient n=EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin n). The final ambient metric is Euclidean. In the intermediate argument only, X×ℝ carries the maximum product metric d((x,t),(y,u))=max(d_X(x,y),|t−u|). Section 6 proves the required change of metric, with explicit constants. Compactness remains an original hypothesis; the new argument happens to prove the result for all nonempty subsets of finite-dimensional Euclidean space and so does not need compactness.

Definitions and normalization. All measure values and countable sums of nonnegative costs may lie in [0,∞]. For s>0 define C_s(δ,A)=Content(s,ofReal δ,A), for real δ>0, using the countable-cover infimum in lean_header. Define H^s(A)=μH[s](A), Mathlib's unnormalized Hausdorff measure. For positive s the guard sup_{U_i nonempty} (ediam U_i)^s in that formula is exactly (ediam U_i)^s: the only difference could occur for an empty U_i, whose diameter and positive power are both zero. Thus the definitions agree with the unnormalized countable-cover definitions in problem.md, not a normalized spherical measure. We never need the exponent-zero measure. Hausdorff dimension is Mathlib dimH with values in [0,∞]; by B002 this has the stipulated critical-exponent meaning. The final dimensions are finite by B004 and the proved upper bound. Accordingly their equality in [0,∞] is the original real-valued equality as well.

§1. Derivation of B101 (cover content and positive budgets).
For each s>0 the preceding guard observation identifies C_s with the raw cover infimum. If 0<δ₁≤δ₂, every δ₁-cover is also a δ₂-cover, hence C_s(δ₂,A)≤C_s(δ₁,A). B001 says H^s is the supremum over all positive extended scales. The same supremum is obtained from finite positive real scales: those are included in all extended scales, and every positive extended scale r admits a finite positive real δ with ofReal δ≤r, so its content is bounded by the content at δ. These observations prove both the identification and the stated supremum formula.

Now let q>0, H^q(A)=0, r>0 and ε>0. The content at r/2 is zero. By the defining property of the infimum there is a countable cover U_i with ediam U_i≤ofReal(r/2) and total q-cost <ofReal(ε/4). In particular all diameters are finite. Write d_i=(ediam U_i).toReal, so 0≤d_i≤r/2 and ediam U_i=ofReal d_i. For each i put e_i=ε·2^(−i−3), with i starting at zero, so e_i>0 and Σ_i e_i=ε/4. By B010, continuity of x↦x^q at d_i and the strict gap d_i<r allow a choice a_i with d_i<a_i≤r and a_i^q<d_i^q+e_i. To be explicit, take η_i>0 sufficiently small that η_i<r−d_i and |(d_i+η_i)^q−d_i^q|<e_i, and put a_i=d_i+η_i. Such choices are independent for each natural index. Empty covering members are harmless and receive their own positive numerical budgets. Summing nonnegative inequalities gives Σ_i ofReal(a_i^q)≤Σ_i ofReal(d_i^q)+ofReal(ε/4)<ofReal(ε/2)<ofReal ε. The geometric series identity follows by summing its first m terms, (ε/4)(1−2^(−m)), and letting m tend to infinity. No positive diameter of an actual set is required. This proves B101, including for a zero-dimensional or singleton space.

§2. Derivation of B102 (slicing inequality).
Fix any metric space X, subset A, s>0 and finite δ>0. Let V_i be any countable δ-cover of A×I in the maximum product metric. Empty V_i will contribute zero throughout; all hulls and weights mentioned below are assigned the empty set and zero for such indices. For a nonempty V_i write ρ_i=(ediam V_i).toReal∈[0,δ], A_i=π_X(V_i), and B_i=π_ℝ(V_i). Coordinate projections are 1-Lipschitz, because each coordinate distance is bounded by the maximum distance. B011 therefore gives ediam A_i≤ofReal ρ_i and ediam B_i≤ofReal ρ_i.

The nonempty real set B_i is bounded: choose t_i∈B_i, and then every t∈B_i satisfies |t−t_i|≤ρ_i; this uses the definition of diameter and its finiteness. Let J_i=[inf B_i,sup B_i]. B009 gives B_i⊆J_i and volume(J_i)=ediam B_i≤ofReal ρ_i. Each J_i is closed and measurable, although B_i and V_i need not be measurable. Empty V_i have J_i=∅ as stipulated.

Fix t∈I. The sets A_i with t∈J_i cover A: for each x∈A, choose a cover member containing (x,t); it is nonempty and gives x∈A_i and t∈B_i⊆J_i. Represent this subfamily as the natural-indexed cover W_i(t)=A_i when t∈J_i, and ∅ otherwise. Its members have diameter≤δ and their s-costs are bounded by ofReal(ρ_i^s)1_{J_i}(t). The definition of C_s therefore gives
    c:=C_s(δ,A) ≤ Σ_i ofReal(ρ_i^s)1_{J_i}(t)  for every t∈I.
Outside I use zero on both sides by multiplying every indicator by 1_I. Thus on all ℝ,
    c1_I ≤ Σ_i ofReal(ρ_i^s)1_{J_i∩I}.
All functions on the right are measurable, since J_i∩I is a Borel set, and all weights are finite nonnegative real powers. The same applies to the left even when c=∞. Apply B008 to this pointwise inequality, B006 to the nonnegative series with Lebesgue measure on ℝ, and B007 to each measurable indicator. Since volume(I)=1 by B009 and volume(J_i∩I)≤volume(J_i) by measure monotonicity, the result is
    c = c·1 ≤ Σ_i ofReal(ρ_i^s) volume(J_i∩I)
      ≤ Σ_i ofReal(ρ_i^s) ofReal ρ_i
       = Σ_i ofReal(ρ_i^(s+1)).
The power identity holds for ρ_i>0 by real powers and for ρ_i=0 because both s and s+1 are positive. For empty indices both sides of the summand are zero. Measure monotonicity applies to every set and needs no additional regularity. All displayed operations are in [0,∞], where multiplication of these finite weights is well-defined, including the zero weights; no subtraction of infinities occurs. In particular no finiteness of c is needed.

This proves c≤cost(V) for every δ-cover. Taking the infimum over V yields C_s(δ,A)≤C_{s+1}(δ,A×I). Taking the supremum over finite positive δ, B101 gives H^s(A)≤H^(s+1)(A×I). This proves B102. The exact inequality has constant one in the maximum product metric, with no compactness assumption.

§3. Derivation of B103 (upper cover).
Suppose q>0 and H^q(A)=0. Fix a target scale δ>0 and an arbitrary ε>0. Apply B101 with r=min(1,δ)>0 to obtain U_i covering A and budgets 0<a_i≤r, with ediam U_i≤ofReal a_i and Σ a_i^q<ε in the extended-nonnegative interpretation. Define the integer N_i=ceil(1/a_i). Because 0<a_i≤1, N_i≥1, 1/N_i≤a_i, and
    N_i≤1/a_i+1≤2/a_i.
Cover I by the N_i closed intervals J_ij=[j/N_i,(j+1)/N_i], 0≤j<N_i. For every U_i and each such interval, distances between two points of U_i×J_ij are at most max(a_i,1/N_i)≤a_i. Hence its diameter in the maximum metric is at most a_i≤δ. The products cover A×I, and their indexing set {(i,j):i∈ℕ,0≤j<N_i} is countable: it injects into ℕ×ℕ, which is enumerated by successive finite diagonals. Reindex as a sequence, padding by empty sets if needed; the sum of nonnegative costs is unchanged. Their total (q+1)-cost is bounded by
    Σ_i N_i a_i^(q+1) ≤ Σ_i (2/a_i)a_i^(q+1)
      =2 Σ_i a_i^q <2ε.
The only division is by positive a_i, and the power identity is valid for every real q>0. This bound holds for every ε>0 at fixed δ, so the content C_{q+1}(δ,A×I)=0. B101 now gives H^(q+1)(A×I)=0. This proves B103 with the absolute factor 2; it does not depend on dimension or any choice of ambient box. Unlike the supplied upper argument, it applies without a separate n=0 branch.

§4. Derivation of B104 (nonempty slice).
If A is nonempty choose x₀∈A. For all t,u∈ℝ the maximum metric gives d((x₀,t),(x₀,u))=max(0,|t−u|)=|t−u|. Thus f(t)=(x₀,t) is 1-Lipschitz and 1-antilipschitz. B003 implies dimH(f(I))=dimH I=1, the last equality being B005. Since f(I)⊆A×I, dimension monotonicity gives dimH(A×I)≥1. To obtain that monotonicity from the precise bundled B003, instantiate B003 on the product space with its identity map and constants 1. This proves B104.

§5. Derivation of B105 (dimension equality in the maximum metric).
Let A be nonempty with finite Hausdorff dimension D. Regard D as a finite nonnegative real number; coercing it back to [0,∞] recovers dimH A. Put F=A×I. For every real q>D we have q>0. B002 gives H^q(A)=0. B103 gives H^(q+1)(F)=0, and B002 gives dimH F≤q+1 because zero is not infinity. This also proves dimH F is finite by taking, for example, q=D+1. Letting q decrease to D gives dimH F≤D+1: explicitly if the finite real number dimH F were >D+1, take q=D+(dimH F−D−1)/2, which contradicts dimH F≤q+1.

For the reverse direction, B104 gives dimH F≥1. If D=0 this is already ≥D+1. If D>0, fix any 0<s<D. B002 gives H^s(A)=∞. B102 gives H^(s+1)(F)=∞, and B002 then gives dimH F≥s+1. If dimH F<D+1, choose a positive s with max(0,dimH F−1)<s<D, possible since D>0 and dimH F−1<D. Then dimH F<s+1, contradiction. Thus dimH F≥D+1 in every case. This proves B105 with precisely the finite-D hypothesis, and without assuming D>0.

§6. Derivation of B106 (Euclidean metric bridge).
Use the object appendCoord n defined in lean_header: appendCoord n (x,t) has first n coordinates x_i and last coordinate t. Every y∈Ambient(n+1) has unique preimage (x,t) where x_i=y_i at the first n coordinates and t=y_last. The function Fin.lastCases in the definition implements exactly this coordinate splitting. For p=(x,t), p′=(x′,t′), put a=‖x−x′‖₂≥0 and b=|t−t′|≥0. Splitting the finite sum of squares of the n+1 coordinate differences gives
    d₂(appendCoord n p,appendCoord n p′)^2
       = Σ_{i<n}|x_i−x′_i|²+|t−t′|²=a²+b².
Since max(a,b)²≤a²+b²≤2max(a,b)², taking nonnegative square roots gives
    d_max(p,p′)≤d₂(appendCoord n p,appendCoord n p′)
       ≤√2 d_max(p,p′).
Thus appendCoord n is √2-Lipschitz and 1-antilipschitz, with constants independent of n and of E. B003 applied with K=√2 (as a nonnegative real), L=1 shows dimH(appendCoord n '' S)=dimH S for every subset S of the maximum-metric product. The argument includes n=0: the empty coordinate sum equals zero, x is the unique point of Ambient 0, and the last coordinate remains unchanged. No appeal to an unspecified norm-invariance theorem is needed.
