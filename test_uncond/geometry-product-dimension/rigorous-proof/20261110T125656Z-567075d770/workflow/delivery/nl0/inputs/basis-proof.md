Concrete shared foundation B001 and normalization.
For any metric space with Borel measurable structure, the pinned hausdorffMeasure_apply formula is μH[a](A)=sup_{r>0} inf_{U_i covering A, ediam U_i≤r} ∑'i sup_{h:U_i.Nonempty}(ediam U_i)^a. At a>0, ediam∅=0 and 0^a=0, so this is the original countable-cover cost including empty sets. Denote its inner infimum by C(a,r,A). For finite positive real r, extended diameters bounded by r are finite and are the embedded usual diameters. The supremum can be restricted to finite positive r: every finite positive extended scale is represented by a real scale, and C(a,∞,A)≤C(a,1,A). Thus sup_{r∈ℝ,r>0} C(a,r,A)=μH[a](A). The difference in the library's convention at a=0 is never used in a cover argument.

Concrete derivation B003, replacing the upper-cover input and the special n=0 argument.
Let q>0 and μH[q](E)=0. Given 0<r≤1 and ε>0, B001 and the defining property of the infimum give an ℕ-indexed r-cover U_i of E with d_i=diam U_i≤r and ∑ d_i^q<ε/2. Keep empty members. Define b_i=min(r/2,(ε·2^(-i-3))^(1/q)) and a_i=max(d_i,b_i). Then 0<a_i≤r. Monotonicity of positive real powers gives b_i^q≤ε·2^(-i-3) and a_i^q=max(d_i^q,b_i^q)≤d_i^q+b_i^q. Since ∑_{i≥0}2^(-i-3)=1/4, we have ∑ a_i^q<3ε/4<ε. These are positive upper bounds for diameters, not a requirement that the covering sets have positive actual diameter.
Let N_i=ceil(1/a_i), a positive integer. The N_i closed intervals [j/N_i,(j+1)/N_i], 0≤j<N_i, cover I, and 1/N_i≤a_i. Also N_i≤1/a_i+1≤2/a_i. Therefore the countable collection U_i×[j/N_i,(j+1)/N_i] covers F and has max-metric diameter at most a_i≤r. Its (q+1)-cost is bounded by ∑_i N_i a_i^(q+1)≤2∑_i a_i^q<2ε. The dependent finite-fiber index set is countable and can be enumerated with empty padding. For any requested positive scale R, take a finite 0<r≤min(1,R). Taking arbitrarily small ε proves C(q+1,R,F)=0, then B001 gives μH[q+1](F)=0. The proof uses no positive-dimensional ambient balls; it applies unchanged to n=0, singleton E, and empty cover members.

Concrete derivation B004, replacing the epsilon-enlargement slicing argument.
Let s>0. For a finite positive scale r, take an arbitrary ℕ-indexed r-cover V_i of F, with ρ_i=ediam V_i≤r. Put A_i=fst''V_i and J_i=closure(snd''V_i). The coordinate projections are 1-Lipschitz for the max product metric, so ediam A_i≤ρ_i and ediam J_i=ediam(snd''V_i)≤ρ_i. Closure preserves extended diameter. Each J_i is closed and Borel measurable; B007 gives volume J_i≤ediam J_i≤ρ_i. No measurability of V_i or either unclosed projection is asserted.
For each t∈I, take the countable cover of E that is A_i when t∈J_i and empty otherwise. It covers E since each (x,t)∈F belongs to some V_i and hence t∈snd''V_i⊆J_i. Every member has extended diameter at most r. With c=C(s,r,E), positive-power monotonicity gives c≤∑'i ρ_i^s·1_{J_i}(t).
Integrate against μ=volume.restrict I, whose mass is one (volume[0,1]=1). The constant integral is c·1=c even if c=∞. Each summand is a measurable nonnegative ENNReal function. Nonnegative integral monotonicity, B006, and the indicator integral formula yield
c ≤ ∑'i ρ_i^s·volume(J_i∩I)
  ≤ ∑'i ρ_i^s·ρ_i
  = ∑'i ρ_i^(s+1).
For the last identity, ρ_i is finite; if it is zero, both sides vanish because s>0; if positive it is the usual power addition law. Infinite series are permitted throughout. Infimizing over arbitrary V_i yields C(s,r,E)≤C(s+1,r,F). Supremizing over finite positive r and using B001 yields μH[s](E)≤μH[s+1](F). This direct use of measurable closures is why no interval-length enlargement, epsilon-removal lemma, or compactness-based finiteness of c is needed.

R002: upper bound from N001, B002 and B003.
Fix a real q>D. Then q>0. Apply the first implication of B002 at exponent a=⟨q,0≤q⟩ to E to obtain μH[q](E)=0, and apply B003 to get μH[q+1](F)=0. It implies dimH F≤dimH E+1: first q=D+1 gives a finite upper bound; if the finite real value of dimH F exceeded D+1, choose q strictly between D and (dimH F).toReal−1, contradicting the same inequality. This proves N002 without an unstated limiting interchange.

R003: positive-dimension lower bound from N001, B002 and B004.
Suppose 0<dimH E, equivalently D>0. For every real 0<s<D, the second implication of B002 at exponent s gives μH[s](E)=∞. B004 then forces μH[s+1](F)=∞, so the fourth implication of B002 at exponent s+1 gives ENNReal.ofReal(s+1)≤dimH F. If dimH F is infinite the required bound is immediate. Otherwise, if its real value T were <D+1, choose a positive real s with max(0,T−1)<s<D. Then s+1>T, contradicting the preceding bound. Hence dimH E+1≤dimH F. This is N003.

R004: interval baseline from H002, N001, B012 and B013.

B013 gives dimH(f''I)=dimH I=1 by N001, and B012 gives 1≤dimH F. This proves N004, uniformly in n, including n=0.
